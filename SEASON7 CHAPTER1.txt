VOID WALKER — SEASON 7
CHAPTER 1: “PEACE THAT WAS NEVER MEANT TO STAY”

The war had ended… but nothing inside Harris understood what “ended” meant anymore.

For so long his life had been built around destruction, survival, and the endless pressure of the Void pressing against reality itself. So when everything suddenly went quiet, it didn’t feel like peace. It felt like something had been removed from the world and nobody had noticed what was missing yet.

Harris stood in the remains of the battlefield where gods once fell, where the sky itself had cracked open during the final clash against Omega Crimson Eclipse. Now there was only wind moving through broken land. No screams. No collapsing universes. No Void pressure crushing existence.

Only silence.

But Harris knew silence could be more dangerous than war.

Because silence meant waiting.

And waiting always meant something was coming.

He exhaled slowly, forcing his face into calm. Around him, his friends were still recovering from the aftermath. Koji was injured but hiding it behind irritation and jokes. Lunar was exhausted but pretending to be fine. Reku, Roku, Lina, and Mika were all carrying invisible damage that no medicine could fully fix.

And Harlina…

Harlina was the only reason Harris was still standing like a human instead of a broken weapon.

But even she didn’t know how much he was hiding.

Every step Harris took sent sharp pain through his body. The final battle had not left clean wounds—it had left something deeper, something that resisted healing like it refused to accept that the war was truly over. Still, he smiled when they were around. Still acted like he was fine. Still pretended that the past wasn’t slowly eating him alive from the inside.

Because if he stopped pretending…

Everything would collapse again.

That evening, instead of resting like he should have, Harris insisted on taking everyone to the hospital. They all thought it was just routine checkups after a war. None of them questioned it too deeply. Even Koji didn’t realize how serious Harris actually was.

At the hospital, chaos of small injuries, bandages, doctors moving quickly, voices overlapping in tired relief filled the building. Everyone was finally getting treated properly.

But Harris stayed outside the main rooms.

He didn’t sit.

He didn’t rest.

He just stood there quietly like someone waiting for a war that hadn’t arrived yet.

Every now and then his hand would tighten slightly, a silent reaction to pain he refused to acknowledge. His mind was somewhere else entirely. Somewhere far beyond this moment.

Because deep inside him…

Something had started to stir again.

Something he thought was gone forever.

By the time everything finished, night had already fallen over the world. The group slowly separated, returning home one by one, tired but alive. Their laughter was weaker than before, but still present. Still human.

Harris and Harlina walked together alone.

No urgency.

No mission.

No world ending behind them.

Just two survivors trying to understand what comes after survival.

The air between them didn’t need conversation. It never really did. Everything important had already been said during the war when words meant nothing and actions meant everything.

When they reached home, exhaustion finally caught up to both of them. The house felt strangely normal, like it belonged to a version of life that shouldn’t exist for someone like Harris.

Harlina collapsed onto the bed first, completely drained. Harris sat beside her for a long time, just watching her breathe. That simple rhythm was more powerful than anything he had experienced in battle. It grounded him. It reminded him that not everything in existence was meant to be destroyed.

Eventually he lay down beside her.

She shifted in her sleep, unconsciously moving closer to him, like even her body refused to accept distance between them anymore. Harris slowly wrapped his arm around her. His expression softened for a moment he would never admit out loud.

For the first time in a long time…

He wasn’t preparing for death.

He was simply existing.

But even peace had limits for him.

Pain still lived inside his body like a second heartbeat.

And deeper than that…

Something else was waiting.

Morning arrived like a quiet intrusion into their fragile peace. Light slipped through the curtains softly, touching the room like it was afraid to wake the past. Harris woke first, as always.

Harlina was still asleep beside him.

For a moment, he didn’t move. He just looked at her, as if confirming she was real and not something the war had left behind in his imagination.

Then, slowly, carefully, he brushed her hair away from her face.

She stirred slightly, confused and half asleep.

Before she could fully wake up, Harris leaned in and kissed her forehead.

Then her cheek.

Her reaction was immediate.

She froze.

Then turned completely red.

Then buried herself under the blanket like she wanted to disappear from existence itself.

Harris couldn’t stop himself from laughing. Not loudly. Not proudly. But softly, like something fragile had cracked open inside him and let a real emotion slip out.

He got up and walked toward the kitchen to make coffee.

The house was quiet again.

Too quiet.

And that was when everything changed.

As he stood there alone, the world didn’t make any sound—but his mind did.

A pressure entered.

Not physical.

Not emotional.

Something beyond both.

The Third Voice.

And behind it…

The Creator’s presence.

It wasn’t speaking loudly. It didn’t need to. The message formed directly inside his consciousness like reality itself was being rewritten.

“Find it.”

Harris froze completely.

His hand loosened.

The cup slipped from his fingers.

It shattered on the floor.

The sound echoed through the house like a warning that something far bigger than peace had just returned.

And then—

He collapsed.

Harlina rushed down immediately when she heard the sound. She found him on the floor, unresponsive. Panic filled her instantly. She shook him, called his name, splashed water on his face, trying to pull him back.

Nothing worked.

Until her hand accidentally pressed one of his hidden injuries.

Harris snapped awake instantly with a scream that shook the entire room.

Harlina stepped back, shocked, fear mixing with frustration.

“What is wrong with you? Why do you keep hiding everything?!”

Harris tried to respond, tried to lie like always, but this time something inside him cracked.

She slapped him lightly.

“Tell me the truth.”

Silence.

Heavy silence.

Then slowly, Harris lifted his shirt.

The wound underneath was still open in places, deeper than anyone expected, still carrying traces of energy that didn’t belong to this world. Harlina’s expression changed completely.

Fear.

Then anger.

Then concern so deep it almost hurt more than the wound itself.

Without hesitation, she grabbed him and forced him to go to the hospital.

Harris resisted at first when they mentioned injections. Not because of pain—but because something about it triggered an old fear buried too deep to explain. But Harlina didn’t let him escape it. She leaned closer and kissed his cheek softly.

That single action broke his resistance completely.

The injection went in.

And for a moment…

He was human again.

When they returned home later, everything looked normal again from the outside. But inside Harris’s mind, nothing was normal anymore. The Third Voice had changed its tone.

It was no longer a whisper.

It was a warning.

“Find it.”

The words repeated endlessly, as if something inside existence itself was trying to wake him up.

Later, Koji arrived.

The moment he saw Harris taking medicine, he immediately knew something was wrong. Lunar followed soon after. Both of them confronted him, shouting, demanding answers, frustration mixing with concern.

Harris only smiled weakly.

“I’m fine.”

Koji looked at him like he didn’t believe a single word.

Then, suddenly, Koji announced something unexpected.

A trip.

Everyone needed to leave the city for a while. Just a break. Just something normal. Something human.

Harris agreed without resistance.

The next day, while everyone prepared, Harlina went out with the others to buy supplies for the trip. Harris stayed behind alone for a while.

The silence in the house felt different now.

Not peaceful.

Not calm.

Empty.

He walked to the entrance and stood there.

Waiting.

He didn’t know what he was waiting for.

But he waited anyway.

Hours passed.

Then finally…

Harlina returned.

She found him asleep near the gate, whispering her name even in unconsciousness.

Something inside her softened immediately.

She woke him gently.

The moment he saw her, he pulled her into a tight hug like he had been waiting for her for years instead of hours.

“Why are you late?”

She laughed softly.

“Sorry…”

And for a moment…

Everything felt normal again.

The rest of the day passed like a fragile illusion of peace. Shopping, laughter, small arguments, teasing, choosing clothes, preparing for the trip. It all felt like a life they should have had long ago but were only now stealing from fate.

That night, they watched a horror movie together.

Harlina kept hiding behind him during every scary scene, holding him tightly without realizing how still he had become—not because of fear, but because of how real she felt against him.

At some point she noticed and became embarrassed, pushing him lightly while turning red.

“Stop staring at me!”

He only smiled.

Later they cooked together. Ate together. Fed each other like it was the most natural thing in existence. For a while, Harris forgot everything. The Void. The war. The voice.

Everything.

That night they slept holding each other again.

But peace never stayed inside his dreams.

He saw himself in a nightmare.

A version of him destroying everything.

Killing his friends.

Standing alone in a dead universe.

He woke up sweating.

Harlina woke immediately.

“What happened?”

“…just a dream.”

She kissed him softly.

“Sleep.”

Morning came too fast again.

Koji called repeatedly.

They were late.

Harris answered calmly.

“We’re on the way.”

Without hesitation, he lifted Harlina onto his shoulder and ran toward the station while she shouted in embarrassment, her face red, trying to escape but not really wanting to.

Behind them…

Something far beyond their world stirred.

Not the Void.

Not the Eclipse.

Something older.

Something watching.

And inside Harris’s mind…

The Third Voice spoke one final time before silence returned.

“He has started to notice.”
